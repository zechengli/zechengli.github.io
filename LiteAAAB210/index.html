Flow

The modem's own attach (standard LTE)

Before the LiteAAA app can say anything, the OnePlus 13's modem attaches to our cell on its own. This is the standard LTE attach; none of our code takes part. The modem and the LTE core run all of it, and the B210 only carries the radio signal. It is the first of the two authentications in the flow above.

The keys this attach produces, all from the card's K:

K (in the SIM and in the HSS)
 +- CK, IK                      computed by the SIM in step 5
     +- K_ASME                  held by the MME
         +- K_NASint, K_NASenc  protect NAS messages (step 7)
         +- K_eNB               given to the eNB (step 10)
             +- K_RRCint, K_RRCenc   protect RRC messages (step 11)
             +- K_UPenc              ciphers user data on the radio link

Where each part runs:

PartRuns on
UE side: RRC, NAS, the radio protocolsthe OnePlus 13's modem (its baseband chip)
The SIM's AKA (step 5)the SIM card
Radio signal at Band 3the B210
eNB: RRC, PDCP, RLC, MAC, PHYsoftware on the Linux PC
MME, HSS, SGW, PGWthe LTE core on the Linux PC

What LiteAAA gets from this: an IP address on our cell and a data radio bearer. The LiteAAA app then opens a plain TCP connection to the PC (10.45.0.1, port 9999) over that bearer, and its own paging, challenge and nonce travel as ordinary user data. So LiteAAA's AKA runs on the user plane, not on RRC or NAS. The radio link ciphers it with KUPenc; LTE does not integrity protect user data.
